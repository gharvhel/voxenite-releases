Privacy

To play Voxenite you make an account with an email address and a password. There are no ads and no tracking, your worlds stay on your PC, and a report reaches us only if you press Send. This page covers the game and voxenite.com.

Who we are

Voxenite is made by Gharvhel Carré, an independent developer, in Florida, USA. For anything on this page, email voxenitegame@gmail.com.

In the game

Your saves stay on your PC. We don't collect them, and the game has no ads or analytics.

Your account. Registering in the game gives us:

  • your email address;
  • a scrambled form of your password, made on your PC, so the password itself never reaches us;
  • whether you have a licence, what kind, and which key you used;
  • when the account was made and when the game last checked in.

Proving your email. When you register, and when you ask to reset a forgotten password, we email you a 6-digit code to enter in the game, so nobody can use an address that isn't theirs. Amazon Web Services sends these emails for us. If someone registers with an address that already has an account, or asks to reset the password of an address that has none, we email that address to say so instead. These are the only emails the game sends. Until you enter the code, what you typed waits on our server for up to 15 minutes: scrambled forms of the address, the password and the code, and the key if you gave one.

If we gave you a key, we note on our own computer who it went to, so we can sort it out if it gets lost.

Checking your licence. When the game starts it asks our server whether your licence is still good. The server sees your IP address while that happens, as any server does, and we don't keep it. Amazon Web Services, which runs that server for us, may keep its own short security logs. To slow down anyone guessing keys or passwords, a scrambled form of your IP address is kept when you register, ask for or enter an email code, enter a key, get a password wrong or start the game signed in, and a scrambled form of your email address when we email you a code or someone gets its password wrong. It normally goes within the hour, or within a few days at most if the server is very busy.

Feedback reports go only if you press Send. A report holds:

  • the note you wrote and a picture of your screen;
  • your account's email address, so we know who sent it and can reply;
  • the game's version, where you were in the world and how the game was running;
  • PC details such as your graphics card and memory;
  • the game's log, with names, email addresses and IP addresses taken out first.

Our server also sees your IP address while a report comes in, as any server does. We don't store it with the report. Press Keep Here and nothing leaves your PC.

Crash files stay in the game's crashes folder until you choose to send them to us.

Updates. The game asks GitHub, where our downloads live, whether a newer version exists, and downloads it if so. Those requests carry your IP address and the version you have, and nothing about you or your world.

Buying the game: Paddle takes your order as Merchant of Record. Paddle holds your name, email address and payment details under its own privacy policy, and tells us your email address, country and what you bought so we can help with your order. We never see your card.

Playing together. Co-op connects your PC to the host's, so the people you play with can see your IP address and your character. We aren't part of that connection.

On this website

voxenite.com sets no cookies. It counts visits and presses of the Buy and download buttons with GoatCounter, which keeps no cookies and nothing that identifies you: only the page, where you came from (such as a link on TikTok), and your browser, screen size and country, in totals.

  • Hosting: GitHub hosts the site and the game's download, and logs visits, including IP addresses, for security.
  • Fonts: the pages load fonts from Google, which sees your IP address.
  • Email list: if you sign up, Kit holds your email address so we can send you the launch email and occasional news. Every email has an unsubscribe link.

Our Discord and social pages are run by those companies under their own privacy policies.

Why we use it, and who sees it

Reports and crash files are used only to fix and improve the game. Your account's email address is used only to run your account and to answer you, and an address on the email list only for the emails you signed up for. We don't sell any of it, and we don't use it for advertising.

Only the Voxenite team reads reports. A picture from a report is never made public without your say-so. The companies that hold data for us are Amazon Web Services (accounts, licences, reports and the code emails), GitHub (the website, downloads and updates), Google (fonts) and Kit (the email list). They are based in the United States, so your data may be handled there.

How long we keep it

  • Your account stays until you ask us to delete it. Deleting it ends its licence, and that key can't be used again: we keep a record that the key was used, with nothing linking it to you. Reports you sent are kept as below unless you ask us to delete those too.
  • An email code, and what you typed with it, stop working when used or after 15 minutes, and are deleted from the server within a few days at most.
  • Reports wait on Amazon Web Services' servers for up to 30 days until we collect them, then move to our own computers.
  • We keep reports and crash files for up to 12 months, then delete them.
  • Your email address stays on the list until you unsubscribe or ask us to remove it.

Your choices

Email us and we'll tell you what we hold about you, correct it or delete it. You don't need to give a reason, and wherever you live you get the same answer. Write from your account's email address, so we know it's you.

If you're in the EU or UK, we hold your account because it's needed to give you the game you asked for, and we rely on your consent for reports and the email list, which you can withdraw at any time. You can complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Voxenite isn't for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data. If you learn a child under 13 has sent us something, email us and we'll delete it.

Changes

If this page changes, the date at the top changes too. If the game ever starts collecting something new, we'll say so here first, and in the game.
